Stage context

Start with the life they are building.

This stage is about establishing financial habits while responsibilities and options are still changing quickly. Excellent looks like accessible emergency savings, full use of any employer match, and protection sized to real obligations.

“Most people your age either have zero coverage or way too little — let's see which one you are.”

Conversation goal

Lead with a quick, tangible win.

Starting the emergency fund and capturing the full employer match.

Priority 1

Protection red

If income stops today (death, disability, illness), does the family survive intact?

What is at risk: Loss of income becomes loss of home, school, stability

Protection addresses the financial consequences of death, disability, illness, and other interruptions to earned income. Strong protection lets a household preserve its day-to-day stability when work cannot continue.

Priority 2

Liquidity yellow

Can a $2,000-$10,000 surprise be absorbed without debt or derailing goals?

What is at risk: Short-term emergencies become long-term debt

Liquidity is accessible money for unexpected costs and near-term needs. It keeps a surprise expense from becoming high-interest debt or an interruption to longer-term saving.

Priority 3

Accumulation yellow

Is money being systematically built for known future costs (education, home, major goals)?

What is at risk: Kids' education gets funded by parents' retirement

Accumulation is the disciplined building of funds for known future goals. It separates education, housing, and other major costs from retirement money.

Listen for this

Common concern, useful reframe.

“I don't need life insurance, I don't have kids.”

Protection here is about debt, co-signers, or a partner who depends on income — and locking in low rates while young and healthy.

Options to explore

Category-level next steps.

Brokerage/managed accounts

Provides a flexible category for non-tax-advantaged goals that do not fit a dedicated education or retirement vehicle.

Cash value in permanent life

May have a secondary liquidity role in an appropriately designed permanent-life strategy; it should not replace a dedicated emergency reserve.

Disability income insurance

Helps address an often-overlooked risk: the loss of ability to earn income, which is more likely during working years than death for many households.

529/education savings vehicles

Creates a dedicated education-funding path so college choices do not automatically draw from retirement savings.

Actual recommendations require a full needs analysis and current licensed product/carrier options — this is a category guide only.

Options to explore

Category-level next steps.

High-yield/interest-bearing emergency account

Separates emergency reserves from daily spending while keeping funds accessible for genuine surprises.

Roth/Traditional IRA and employer plans

Supports tax-advantaged long-term saving; unmatched employer contributions should be flagged as a priority to review.

Permanent life insurance

May be considered after term protection needs are covered when long-duration protection or other policy features fit the broader plan.

Structured savings habit

Uses automatic transfers and sinking funds to make consistent saving a behavior, rather than relying on a particular vehicle.

Actual recommendations require a full needs analysis and current licensed product/carrier options — this is a category guide only.

Options to explore

Category-level next steps.

Supplemental health / critical illness

Can help a household plan for costs and disruption associated with serious illness beyond standard medical coverage.

Term life insurance

Often the default first move for temporary income-replacement needs, commonly evaluated in the range of 10-20x income replacement.
